## Community description

Ethics4NLP is an open interest group on ethics in research and teaching in natural language processing (NLP), established in 2023 following discussions at Swedish language technology *ämnesdagar*. The initiative brings together researchers, teachers, students and practitioners from language technology and related disciplines to exchange experiences, ideas and educational materials.

## Purpose and aims

As language technologies become more widely used, their performance alone is insufficient to establish their suitability for particular applications. We aim to promote ethically aware research, development and use of NLP, and to support the integration of ethical considerations into language technology education.

Topics of interest include:

- Bias, fairness and representation in training data and computational models.
- Privacy, personal integrity, anonymisation and pseudonymisation.
- Intellectual property rights and responsible data collection and use.
- Language technology for identifying harmful language use.
- Linguistic diversity and applications in under-resourced domains and communities.
- Legal, philosophical, societal and environmental implications of language technology.
- Ethical evaluation of NLP systems and the development of teaching methods and resources.

## Previous workshops

[The first Ethics4NLP workshop](https://gu-clasp.github.io/language-and-perception/events/ethics-for-nlp/) took place on 23 January 2024 at the University of Gothenburg. It brought together more than 30 participants to discuss ethical challenges in NLP research and approaches to teaching ethics. Participants agreed to work towards a collaborative network and a shared bank of educational resources, with the longer-term ambition of developing a short online course.

[The second Ethics4NLP workshop](https://gu-alpine.github.io/ethics-for-nlp-2026/index.html), on 25 November 2026, continued this research and educational agenda. Its [programme](https://gu-alpine.github.io/ethics-for-nlp-2026/schedule.html) focused on benchmark evaluation, privacy-preserving research and practical approaches to teaching ethics in NLP.
